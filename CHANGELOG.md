# Changelog

## 1.1.2 · 2026-11-21

What people wear follows the story, and a few fixes. Your saves open as they are: no save format change.

### What people wear

- **Outfits follow the story.** When a reply clearly has someone undress, half-undress, dress again or change clothes,
  their picture follows and the AI is told. Undressing happens only in a private place with adult content on, and
  changing into another outfit only where they can change (their home). It costs one short AI call after a reply, and
  only when someone in it could change; a new setting, **Outfits follow the story** (☰ → This world → World), turns it
  off.
- **More of Tokyo's outfits can be put on as the story goes,** each saying what it is so the AI knows how it looks:
  Tamaki's off-duty, opening-night and home clothes, Ayame's gala gown and night out, Kaede's road-trip and night-out
  clothes (her road-trip clothes can be her clothes at the van too), and Momo's volleyball jersey. Kanon's old T-shirt
  is what's left of her own clothes, and Kaoru's plain one-piece is her beach cover-up with the shirt off.
- **World makers:** an outfit can say what it opens from, in the World creator.

### Fixes

- The Calendar's badge no longer counts a city event you've already been to while it's still going on.
- Collection: Tokyo's keepsake kinds are spelled right (Plushies, Accessories, Flowers), their hint no longer repeats
  the name in brackets, and a group's progress bar and count run the full width of the row.
- Everything new in Portuguese and Spanish.

## 1.1.1 · 2026-11-20

Small changes after 1.1.0: Spark shows real profiles, the Calendar can wait until it's time to go, people's pages open
from their faces, you learn someone's job by seeing them at it, and you no longer pick what other people wear. Your
saves open as they are: no save format change.

### Spark

- **Real profiles:** a first name and age, what they wrote in their own words, the app's questions they answered, and
  one to six photos of them at the places they go, in outfits that fit (adult ones only with adult content on). No
  surname, no job title, nothing they keep secret. Every built-in person on the app has one; anyone else gets one from
  the AI the first time they're shown, kept in the save.
- **Only people who would really put up a profile are on it.** In Tokyo, sixteen are; nineteen aren't the kind (the
  idol whose agency forbids it, the secret couple, the host and the hostess, people who would be recognized…). Nobody
  who refuses every photo is on it, and someone who gets together with anyone leaves it unless they're open to more.
- **Spark is for dating:** Friendship is gone from what you can look for (a Friendship picked in an existing game is
  dropped), and nobody's photos go further than they would in public.

### The phone and the Calendar

- **Wait and go:** a plan or an event you said you'd go to can wait in the Calendar until it's time to leave, then
  make the trip so you arrive as it starts. It asks first, with what the wait would make you miss (a shift, another
  plan, the place closing, someone going their own way). The clock's "Let time pass?" offers the same.
- **The home screen** has bigger icons under a Today card (the time, the day and what's next, opening the Calendar),
  and the closed phone in the corner peeks in the new style. The bar at the bottom closes the phone from the home
  screen.
- **Homes is in tabs** (Your home, For rent, For sale), Campus and Jobs show what's waiting for you to choose, the
  Shop says when something can be given as a gift, the Calendar shows each event's and festival's own emoji, and
  Collection's groups stay closed once you close them.
- **Rows that scroll sideways** (tabs, quick replies) can be dragged or wheeled with a mouse, and the wheel over them no
  longer scrolls the page.

### People

- **A face opens their page:** a face in Here or With you, or Their card on a sprite, opens that person's page in
  People; someone you haven't met gets a short page ("You don't know them yet"). The scene card is gone: what you can
  do with someone stays in the menu you get by clicking them.
- **You no longer pick what other people wear:** the sprite menu has no outfits, and anyone left in one nothing calls
  for changes back. At Home, a room's dress code is followed only as far as each person's comfort goes (set on your own
  cards in Library › Expressions), and never undresses anyone.
- **You learn what someone does for a living** by seeing them at work, working the same job or getting to know them
  (Acquaintance), not just from saying hi; family and whoever lives with you you'd know anyway, and once learned it
  stays learned. Their page says when you don't know yet, and a good word at their job needs knowing it. In an existing
  game, people you've only said hi to are learned again the same way.
- **Play as is off** unless you turn it on (Settings → Game); someone already playing another person can still go back
  to their own character.
- **Suggested life goals fit where you are:** your start's own first, then what fits who you are and what you're doing.

### World makers

- The World creator edits a person's dating profile ("On the dating app", or "Not the kind to have a dating
  profile") and a festival's icon.

### Other changes

- Everything new in Portuguese and Spanish.

## 1.1.0 · 2026-11-20

Through the lens, and at home in Tokyo. Tokyo has a photographer's career, from Studio MIZU's white basement to a
freelance name with a studio and photobooks of your own, and everyone gets Lens for their photos. The Kōenji share
house has rooms inside, a friend can stay over for a few days, and people dress for where they are: swimwear at
Odaiba's summer beach and two new places to swim, yukata at the festivals. The phone is new from end to end, who you
are counts for more when you make your character, and with adult content on, nine of Tokyo's women have much more to
them. Habits are gone. Your saves are upgraded once (save
format 9), with a backup kept in the save.

### A photographer's life

- **Lens, for everyone:** a phone app that shows the photos you take as pictures of the place as it was (its hour,
  weather and season) and the people in the frame, with the camera's look, your portfolio first, a photo large and
  starred, and your photo keepsakes.
- **A small board of photo gigs** opens with a camera of your own: a stock agency's request, a café's new menu, a
  friend's portrait, each with its place, its window, a deadline and its pay.
- **Studio MIZU**, off Cat Street, and **Erika Mizushima**, the photographer who runs it, with her portrait, 28
  expressions and a storyline, *The white basement*. Assist her through three studio ranks, then **go freelance** in
  Editorial, Weddings and events or Studio portraits (and, with adult content on, Gravure and adult). Freelance has no
  shifts: you're paid by the brief, with a retainer on Mondays (¥40,000 to ¥56,000 a week) for a week with a delivery,
  and how you leave the studio, with Erika's blessing or after a falling-out, is remembered. A branch's top takes about
  two game years.
- **Briefs, start to finish:** offered by your reputation, your contacts, your skills and who will stand in front of a
  camera, each with a window of light, weather and day (*a clear afternoon*, *at night in the rain*, *while Sanja
  Matsuri is on*) and a deadline long enough for its weather to come, extended once if it never did. Set the shoot with
  its subject, **Shoot the assignment** with every part of the quality named (the camera, a lens, the flash, the light,
  an assistant, how the subject feels about you, the studio's lights), take it again once, **Edit today's shoots**, and
  **Deliver** for the client's stars, the pay and reputation. Twenty clients in Tokyo; contacts keep calling after you
  quit.
- **A photo studio of your own:** rent or buy one of three in the Homes app (a basement in Ura-Harajuku, a warehouse
  floor by the bay, a top floor in Shimokitazawa), fit it with a lighting rig, a cyclorama, a dressing room or a
  retouching desk, or rent Studio MIZU by the hour. A storyline can end by handing you one.
- **Photobooks:** five, from Kōyōsha once you have a name, published as keepsakes with royalties that fade over about
  two game years. New gear in the shop: three lenses, a flash, a drone and a waterproof housing.
- **Weddings on a set day.** The world's engaged couples now pick the first weekend a week or two ahead, the news says
  when, and they marry on it (a split before the day calls it off). A photographer who knows them is asked to shoot it.
- **People's rule in front of a camera:** some won't be photographed, some only from behind or only for work, and
  some ask first, in your snapshots too. A rule can loosen as a game goes on, and you're told when it does: Tamaki sits
  for your camera at her last tier and, with adult content on, Kaoru agrees once her notebook ends well.
- A new life goal, **Through the lens**.

### At home

- **Rooms inside a home.** The Kōenji share house has a common room, a shared kitchen, a bathroom and a bedroom each for
  you, Mio, Ren, Chloe and Haru, each with its pictures by day, afternoon and night, its own things to do and its own
  words for the AI. Walking between them takes no time, and the scene is the room you're in.
- **Housemates keep the house's rhythm:** in bed at night, the kitchen at meals, the common room in the evening. They
  come to find you now and then, and their bedroom doors open to close friends. "Elsewhere at home" shows who is where,
  a party at home spreads over the rooms, and Keep to myself keeps housemates away too.
- **A friend can stay over:** ask someone close (friendship 50, or your partner) from their card or their page in
  People to stay one to three nights, or for a while (a week at most), on the sofa or in your room. They ring the
  doorbell that evening, keep their own job and days, grow a little closer each night, and go home on the last
  morning. The Calendar shows who is staying. Not in a Home.

### Dressing for the place

- **People change for where they are,** as bold as they go in public, a step bolder in private with someone close,
  and not the same set every day. The AI is told who wears what, and a sprite's menu lists their outfits.
- **Swimwear for 37 of Tokyo's people:** 85 sets from a modest one-piece to a string bikini, each with its
  expressions (the bold ones only with adult content on).
- **Odaiba's beach is open for swimming** in summer, drawn again with its summer look, and there are **two new places
  to swim**: the **Azabu Terrace Night Pool**, a hotel's rooftop pool in Roppongi on summer evenings (¥6,000: the pool,
  a bar and a DJ), and **Hoshizora Spa Park** in Hakone, a swimsuit hot spring open all year (¥2,800: pools down the
  hill, a slide, a cave bath), with their pictures in the rain, the snow and autumn.
- **Outfits for the occasion:** Tamaki and Yui in yukata at the summer festivals, Ayame in her fencing whites at the
  Kudan club, Kaede in her jumper at her van. Eight more outfit sets come with Tokyo.

### The phone

- **Bigger and frameless.** It unfolds to twice its width, showing a list beside the conversation or the person, and
  one **Back** button goes back a step at a time.
- **Messages** has the town's news at the top: **Chirp is gone**. A text that asks to meet is an invitation, busy
  people answer later, quick replies fit the moment, and a heart on a message lands well or, on bad news, badly.
- **People** has filters (free now, friends, romance, family, at home, work, neighbours) and a dot on each picture;
  a person's page has tabs: About, Memories, Circle and Notes.
- **The Calendar is an agenda:** the next seven days in a strip, and the chosen day with everything in it in time
  order. **The Profile** is in tabs (You, Skills, Courses, Perks), and **Jobs** has Your job and Openings.
- **Money** pays the bills, and Jobs, Campus, Business and Lens share one Work icon.

### Who you are

- **A strength adds to your start.** When your start already gives you some of a skill, a strength raises it two
  levels more, so it's never wasted: Musical after *Back after years away* makes Music 5, and the chip says so
  ("Musical · Music 3 → 5").
- **One strength for each of Tokyo's skills:** Good singer, Good dancer, Way with words, Fashionable, Gamer, Good eye
  (Photography), Bilingual (English or Japanese, whichever your start gives you less of) and Good bartender (Mixology)
  are new. Strong, Handy, Good with numbers and Tech-savvy are no longer offered, as they said the same as Athletic,
  Smart and Gamer; a persona that has them still converts.
- **Every personality trait has a cost,** so no choice is simply best: Calm, Cheerful, Romantic, Curious and Kind now
  give something up too, and Curious trains skills 10% faster up to level 5 (it was 15% up to level 3). Opposites
  can't be picked together (Outgoing and Shy, Calm and Hot-headed, Ambitious and Lazy, Polite and Sarcastic). Tokyo
  adds **Polite**, **Night owl** and **Studious**, each liked by a few jobs.
- **Every interest counts in Tokyo:** Travel is a day in Yokohama or Hakone, Nightlife a night in Golden Gai,
  Kabukichō, Roppongi or Noge, Art the museums too, Books Jinbōchō and the university, Anime & manga Akihabara. Two
  new interests: **Nature** and **Photography**.
- **Your household shows who your start has you living with,** instead of "You live alone for now", and the
  Households step only appears when you bring characters of your own.
- Your existing games keep the character you made; the traits' new costs count from now on.

### Adult content (adult content only)

- **Sumire, Lisa, Tsubaki, Kanon, Momo, Airi, Shiori, Yui and Kaoru** have 26 new storylines between them, from
  Sumire's Fridays and Lisa's own studio to Shiori's request box and Kaoru's rooms, each with its tile in Tokyo memories,
  and Tokyo's private moments go from 17 to 134.
- **Rooms of their own:** a night at a love hotel shows its room, Lounge Velvet's VIP room, Coco Palace's themed rooms
  and Shiori's closet studio show theirs while the scene plays, and tea or pillow talk afterwards stays in that room.
- **Getting caught with someone** has their own words wherever it happens: who saw, how they take it, a change in how
  they feel about you and a rumor. Caught at a home, nobody bans you, but word gets around.
- Sumire, Lisa, Tsubaki and Momo at the night pool, the spa park and the beach, a boudoir sitting in your studio, and
  Lisa's studio poster on the small photo board.

### Habits are gone

- Doing something often no longer turns into a trait (Gym regular, Early riser…): no morning mood or energy, no
  faster learning, no Habits panel. Life goals' perks reward what you work at, and a place still knows you as a regular.
  Your existing games lose the habits they had.

### World makers

- **The World creator** has a Photography tab (the rules, both boards, clients, briefs and photobooks), photo gear on
  shop items and fixtures, photo studios in Homes & rent, freelance paths, a person's rule in front of a camera and how
  it loosens, and an ending that gives a photo studio.
- **Rooms inside** a kind of home or a housing area; **Outfits for the place** on places, things to do, street
  stories, festivals and standing appointments, with outfits waiting for their pictures marked; a place closed for
  whole seasons.
- **A person's own door:** their home kept closed for good, until something has happened, or only with adult content
  on, with their own words at the door. **Getting caught** words for a place, a home or a person.
- **A thing to do's own background**, by part of the day: it shows while the scene plays, can keep the room it's in,
  and can have a picture for each place it's played at.
- **Character creation:** which traits can't go with each other ("Can't go with"), and strengths that raise only the
  lowest of their skills (Bilingual).
- New systems you can switch off: Photography, Rooms at home, Outfits for the place and Busy people.

### Other changes

- Background AI calls (the Expressions classifier in Full Context mode, a summary, /gen) no longer count as turns of
  the scene, so a question you asked waits for the real answer.
- Help explains rooms at home, friends staying over, photography, dressing for the place and the new phone.
- Everything new in Portuguese and Spanish.

## 1.0.0 · 2026-11-17

Tokyo's long game. Storylines now start once you know someone and wait for you at their end, and once rent stops
being the worry there is a whole end game to aim for: a shop of about 160 things, homes you buy, fit out and let, a
record of everything you've lived in Tokyo, and a museum of your own in an old Yanaka storehouse, with its curator
Tamaki, a rival collector, a dealer in a camper van, halls for nature and for before Edo, royalties, a short story
called The copyist and ten life goals of its own. Natsuki, Lisa and Yui get storylines, an overlay someone shares
installs from a zip, and a new game opens on Tokyo. Your saves are upgraded once (save formats 7 and 8), with a
backup kept in the save.

### Storylines that wait for you

- **They start once you know someone.** A storyline never starts for someone you haven't met. Most start when you
  become friends; eight start as soon as you've met (Mio, Kaito, Ayaka, Camille, Linh, Hannah, Momo and Kanon),
  colleagues' once you work the same job, and a few with a business of yours. Learning someone's secret any other way
  starts it too. Tokyo has 43, for 32 of its people (13 of them with adult content on).
- **They wait for you at the end.** A storyline runs through the seasons on its own time, and when its last stage is
  over, the next time you're together they bring it up. A card asks you: **Help**, **Wait** for a better ending (with
  what each good way still needs) or **Let it be**. Two stories have a price for Help: lend Kaito what he owes, or put
  up the key money for Genzō's new lease.
- **Last chances.** If you wait, they come back about once a season, in person or by a text you answer in the phone
  (Meet them, Help or Not now), at most two such texts a week. Three missed and the story ends without you, and never
  well: its good endings need you there. A few whose bad ending is only things going on as they are (Kanon, Hannah,
  Seo-yeon, Tsubaki, Sumire, Kaoru) cool off a friendship level instead, and wait for you once more.
- **Stories that hang on each other** wait quietly for the other to end (Genzō's lease on Ayaka's contest, Jake on
  Rina's company). Someone leaving Tokyo goes the morning after the deciding scene, so the goodbye is played with you.
- **Their story**, on a person's page in People once you know their secret, says what it's called, where it stands and
  what would help; **Talk about it** brings the card back whenever you're with them. Help has a topic, Storylines.
- **New storylines for Natsuki, Lisa and Yui:** NATSU-P and the rhythm-game finals at Pixel Castle, with duets and a
  word for Hina; Lisa dancing on a torn shoulder, with physio at Seiwa General and her Sunday crowd; Moon Fox's finale
  on the night of the shrine's moon kagura, with writing afternoons in Jinbōchō and a paperback at the book festival.
- **A secret learned and a story you followed to its end** give 20 life points each. Rina's Mochi Byte deal waits for
  you while you work at Mochi Byte as her friend, at any rank.
- **Your existing games** keep what they had: a storyline already over stays over, one under way with someone you've
  met keeps its stage and ends in a scene with you, and one with someone you haven't met starts again when you get to
  know them (a secret you learned stays learned).

### The shop

- **About 160 things in ten categories**, from a ¥1,000 charm to a ¥15,000,000 car with a driver, each a tab, with
  **Here** for what the place you're at sells and **Yours** for what you have. Most come online; some are sold only at
  their places (knives at Tsukiji, watches and cars on Ginza Chūō-dōri, a shamisen at Sensō-ji), and some only by
  invitation.
- **What they do:** small bonuses where only the best one counts (a skill that trains faster, a better morning, a
  first impression), services that last some days, over thirty new things to do, and a car that drives you home after
  the last train. The AI is told the dearest things people can see on you and what's in your home, never what they
  cost or do.
- **Photography is a skill:** ten of Tokyo's best views from the first day, a camera to make it come faster, a roll
  developed at home, photos to sell.
- **Gifts to give** are bought as keepsakes, to give when you like. A gift's worth is still how well it fits, and some
  people can't stand one (Tamaki, and new things made to look old).
- **Two new places:** Kamome Marina in Yokohama and Akatsuki Motorworks behind Azabu's embassy streets. With adult
  content on, an Adult tab.

### A home of your own

- **Buy a home** to live in or as a second home: at the Toyosu towers or the Azabu residences, or one of four that are
  only for sale, each with its pictures: a Roppongi penthouse, a hot-spring villa above Lake Ashi in Hakone, a
  Den-en-chōfu mansion and a 1920s villa in Yamate, Yokohama. A weekly upkeep instead of rent; never repossessed; sold
  back for part of what you paid.
- **Rooms and fixtures:** 24 of them, from a yoga room to a grand piano, with things to do at home. Four are rooms of
  their own on the map: the penthouse's rooftop terrace, the Yamate villa's veranda, the mansion's cypress bath and the
  Hakone villa's open-air bath, under snow in winter.
- **Let the homes you don't live in** (at Toyosu and Azabu, three at most): an agent finds a tenant about three weeks in
  four and pays you the rent less a fifth each Monday. A week's notice has it back.
- **Parties sized to the home**, up to 30 guests, with a caterer or a chef and staff.
- **The two top rents went up** to fit homes that can be bought: the Toyosu towers from ¥60,000 to ¥90,000 a week, the
  Azabu residences from ¥110,000 to ¥180,000. The Family money start keeps its flat at ¥60,000, and a game renting
  there is told once.

### Tokyo memories, and a museum of your own

- **Tokyo memories**, in the phone's new Collection app: a record that fills by itself, a slot for every storyline
  (whichever way it ended), festival, city event, district, life-goal tier, business at its top and keepsake kind.
  Only what your game can have counts, and nothing is ever taken away; a game already running starts it with what it
  remembers.
- **A museum of your own:** about 100 pieces of art, crafts and antiques and Modern Tokyo (and the night world, with
  adult content on), from finds, shops, festival stalls, people's storylines, a weekly dealer and four sales a year.
  Start with glass cases at home, then take on the Kurahashi kura in Yanaka and grow it with wings and upgrades,
  renown, visitors, seasonal shows and a workshop that restores damaged pieces and makes replicas.
- **Tamaki Kurahashi**, its curator, an appraiser who was a child star of a TV show, lets you closer in five tiers as
  the museum fills, and has four storylines of her own.
- **Ayame Mikage**, a rival collector who bids against you at the Ginza sales, lets you closer (heat, never friendship)
  with every lot you take from her: her foundation's gala, her fencing club, the Mikage house, her grandfather's scroll
  and her grandmother's irises, loans of your pieces for a season and six paid authentication cases. **Kaede Utsumi**,
  the dealer whose camper van parks in Yanaka, at Yokohama's harbour and in Hakone, warms to you with what you buy.
  Both have their pictures and storylines.
- **Nature and Before Edo:** insects, fish and pressed flowers by season and hour in the parks, Hakone and the bay,
  with a net, a rod and a flower press in the shop; Hijiri University's autumn dig, its finds on loan, and Naumann's
  elephant, found a part at a time and mounted in the kura.
- **Royalties** each Monday once the kura is yours, by how complete it is: ¥10,000 a week at 10%, up to ¥150,000 at
  100%, with postcards, a book of the prints, a documentary and Japan Post's stamps along the way.
- **The copyist**, a short story for the end game: fakes in Kaede's van, traced with Tamaki and Ayame to old Sekine,
  the Yanaka mounter who painted the Mikage Sesshū, in five scenes. What you do with his pattern book moves all three
  women's trust and heat and never costs money; four fakes go in a case of their own. With adult content on, a night
  with each of them.

### Life goals for the end game

- **Ten new goals for Tokyo**, each offered once its part of the game is in reach: House proud, Landlord, The good
  life, A café to remember, Hotelier, The corner office, Chronicler of Tokyo, Natural history, Every hall and, with
  adult content on, Collector of the night. Each ends with a keepsake and a perk that makes a number grow.
- **Patron of the arts' perk** now raises the museum's renown by 10% instead of lowering its prices; a game that had it
  is told once.
- **Eight new rewards** in the shop (a dealer's tip, an early catalogue, Ebisu's charm, a field guide, Press day, the
  workshop's priority, a party planner, a good tenant), and life points for a museum hall complete, each quarter of
  Tokyo memories, a first home and a business at its top.

### Starting a game

- **Tokyo leads the way in:** the main menu's slideshow shows Tokyo's maps, and New game › World lists Tokyo first,
  already chosen.
- **Adult content is asked.** It is still off by default; when a world has some, a window asks, and the answer can be
  remembered for new games (Settings → Game: ask, off or on).

### World makers

- **The World creator edits all of it:** a storyline's title, earlier ways in, deciding scene, last-chance texts, an
  ending's help, a standing appointment for a stage, and "Can't happen without" (a storyline that needs other people in
  the game besides its own); the shop's items; homes for sale, rooms and fixtures, and letting; the Collection tab,
  with a button that adds a slot for everything the world has; the museum, its pieces, people's tiers, digs and parts;
  the end game's goal conditions and reward kinds.
- **Overlay packs:** an overlay someone shares as a zip installs from Settings → Add-ons, its pictures and all. Mundi
  never ships one.
- **Owning a home** and the **Collection** are systems of their own: switched off, none of it exists. Porto Alto and
  the Shared House play as before.

### Other changes

- Services paid ahead say how long they last, Luxury things a maker ships can be ordered online, and a course's "not"
  requirement is read right.
- Everything new in Portuguese and Spanish.

## 0.4.1 · 2026-11-14

Hotels come to Tokyo, the business end game's third track: a capsule hotel, a ryokan in partnership and, with adult
content on, a love hotel with a night desk. Your saves open as they are: no save format change.

### Hotels

- **Nest Capsule, in Shinjuku.** Rise to Hotel manager and the owner offers it to you, or buy it when it's for sale.
  You pay part of the price at once and the rest comes out of the hotel's takings until it's paid off, never out of
  your own money. Grow it from capsules to a sauna and lounge, then a cabin hotel with a women's floor.
- **Ryokan Kasumi-sō, in Hakone,** isn't for sale: through Shizuka's story her family takes you in as a partner, with
  a third of the takings, half once you fund the open-air bath, then the detached rooms across the garden.
- **Hotel Coco Palace, with adult content on.** Take it over or buy it; keep Kaoru's window or put in the self
  check-in machine; themed floors, then a second hotel in Isezakichō and the owner's suite. The night desk sees who
  comes in with whom: now and then someone you know, when it moves a story. Keep it, use it or let it slip, or tell
  the hotel never to tell you.
- **Rooms and the calendar.** A hotel earns by its rooms: Capacity opens them, its other numbers and the calendar fill
  them (the last train's Fridays and the year-end parties, the autumn leaves and New Year, weekends and Christmas Eve).
  "Work the front desk" passes the hours like a shift. One hotel of each kind, beside a café.
- **People.** Someone may rent a room for a business of their own (Tsubaki's aroma salon). Shizuka's, Kaoru's and
  Tsubaki's stories can end at your hotel; the Hamakaze Group's man comes to your desk, and Sayuri can count on you or
  not; Shiori's family's hotel group is your rival, and would buy you out. A buyer's offer may come for more.
- **World makers:** the World creator's Business tab makes hotels: kinds, rooms and prices, the calendar, buying and
  partnerships, the owner's note, tenants, the night desk and buyers.

### Other changes

- Everything new in Portuguese and Spanish.

## 0.4.0 · 2026-11-13

Running a business comes to Tokyo, as an end game in two tracks: a café of your own, and the executive's floor above a
company's top ranks. Four new women join Tokyo's night world with adult content on, the people around you no longer

### Running a business

## 0.2.1 · 2026-10-28

Fixes and improvements for Home mode.

### Security

Thanks to **coldasblues**, whose fork found these.

- **World files can no longer run code.** Picture addresses, numbers and colors from a world file (yours or one
  you imported) went into the page's styles as they were, and a crafted file could run code inside SillyTavern.
  Now a picture can only be a picture, numbers stay numbers and colors stay colors, and everything Mundi draws
  is cleaned of anything that could run code.
- **Slash commands:** text sent through SillyTavern's commands (lines, narration, read aloud, pictures) can no
  longer carry an STscript `{: … :}` block.
- **Importing a world** only uses the pictures inside the file: nothing is downloaded from other sites.
- **Messages can't press Mundi's buttons:** a reply (or a card, or a shared chat) with HTML that copied Mundi's
  controls could make a click do something in Mundi. Those controls are now removed from what messages show.
- **Pictures from links in messages** now follow SillyTavern's "Forbid external media" (a picture from another
  site tells that site you are reading). To show them anyway: Chat look → Show pictures from links.
- **Every attribute filled from a world, a card or a save is escaped**, also in the world creator's fields, and a
  currency symbol or an icon can't carry HTML.

- **Invite people over** from the Calendar (a new Visits tab). They arrive about an hour later and stay until
  you say goodbye.
- **The doorbell:** when they arrive, it rings. Open the door yourself, or someone else answers after a while.
  If you are out, they are let in.
- **Where they sleep:** a free bedroom, your room or a housemate's room, chosen for each guest (and changeable
  while they stay).
- **Guests' rooms** are always open to you, and saying goodbye in their room no longer leaves you in the dark.

### Parties and going out

### Looks and sound

- **The AI narrates what you do right away** (Things to do, in a scene). On by default.
- **AI calls in one place:** This world → AI context lists everything that makes Mundi call the AI (narrating what
  you do, the diary, characters' texts, Chirp posts and pictures), each with its own switch. New: characters' texts
  can use short ready-made replies instead of the AI.
- **Rain and storm sounds**, with their own switch and volume (Settings → Weather sounds).
- **Cards layout:** sprites on the left or the right.
- **Visual novel:** the dialog box on the left, with an adjustable width.
- **Backgrounds** fade into each other when you move.
- **Character picker:** folders keep their color.

### Home

- **This Home** comes first in the menu and holds the Home settings.
- **Company stays:** someone who comes along with you stays until you part (no timer), and can go to your room
  with you.
- **More kinds of family ties.**
- **Bedroom doors:** someone's room when they're not in is locked (no more ringing a doorbell inside the house); when
  they're in, you knock.
- **The menu** no longer repeats "Go to my room" and "Go out" (they're on the top bar).
- **What you can do fits the room:** eating and drinking in the kitchen (everything) and the dining room (a meal, a
  coffee, a drink, a snack); the TV and the new **Play video games** in the living room; washing up (a shower, the
  new **Brush your teeth**) in the bathrooms. Rooms you set up yourself in the Home creator keep your choice.
- **Things to do in each room:** swim, sunbathe or relax by the pool; do the laundry; tinker or wash the car in the
  garage; sit on the porch or water the plants; tidy up the storage room; set the table; read in bed. Working out
  (the treadmill too) is in the gym.
  Your own Homes get the same: a room called "Piscina", "Garage", "Banheiro"… gets the actions of that kind of room
  (by its name, in English, Portuguese or Spanish).
- **Homes you made before** are brought up to date when they open: they get the actions added since and lose built-in
  ones that no longer exist. Actions you made yourself are never touched, and one you remove stays removed.
- **Go out** from the garage and the entrance too, not only from the floor plan.
- **Go somewhere else:** when you are out, go on to another place (the cinema, then the mall, then a restaurant…)
  with its own name and background; who is with you comes along.
- **Running into someone:** stopping to talk no longer makes them follow you. They stay where you met for a while.
- **New pictures** for Stella and Seraphina in the tour.
- **Needs, if you want them:** This Home → Needs turns on hunger, thirst, mood and hygiene for this game (off by
  default). Their icons show under the clock, they drop with time, and the AI is told when one is low. No energy, and
  nothing bad happens when one runs out.
- **The Advanced switch** at the top of This Home is easier to see.

### First steps, tour and gallery

- **Choose your language first:** the first time Mundi opens, before Stella shows up, a window asks only for the
  language (English, Português (Brasil), Español). The welcome and the tour then come in that language.
- **Stella's tour box** stays in the same place and the same size on every step, so the buttons (Skip tour, back,
  Next) never move. The text is bigger, the step count sits next to the buttons, and Stella is shown whole from the
  waist up, standing on the box's bottom line.
- **Gallery:** two tabs, **Saves** and **Character greetings**. Saves are one grid, newest first, with the game's
  name under each album (before, every game had its own row, so it looked like a list).
- **Settings → Game → What's new** opens this version's changelog instead of the tour.
- **The Old Manor** has a cover picture.

### Fixes

- **Playful slaps and teasing** no longer cost bond: a harsh line only counts if the character takes it badly (the
  emotion of their reply, or, without the Expressions extension, the words of it: they laugh, or they pull away in
  tears). Said with a grin, "de leve", "playfully"… it doesn't count at all.
- **Family and ties:** someone added later as your spouse (or fiancé, or partner) shows as Married (Engaged,
  Dating) and is no longer a stranger. Before, this only worked when set while creating the game.
- **Rooms off the floor plan:** a guest sleeping in a shared room no longer creates a room that isn't on the plan.
- **Scene background:** the floor plan no longer gets stuck in the background after you move.
- **The phone** no longer slides in again when a dialog opens.
- **Exporting a world** now takes along the backgrounds you picked from your own SillyTavern (My backgrounds),
  so the person who imports it sees them too.

## 0.2.0 · 2026-10-25

Everything since 0.1.0 (the last version on GitHub, commit `33837e1`).

### Highlights

- **Home**, a new way to play: one house, the characters you choose, and pure role-play.
- **Porto Alto**:
  - a picture for every place;
  - three kinds of apartments and houses;
  - a new gym and a new cinema.
- **A living world**: people with full days, places that get busy and quiet, day and night, and news from the
  lives of the people you know.
- **The interface in Português (Brasil) and Español.**
- **Save safety**: migrations, automatic backups and branches.
- **The RPG mode and the medieval style** are built but locked ("Soon") while they get finished.

### Home mode (new)

- **What a Home is:** a house and the characters who live in it, with no work, money or bills. Life outside
  happens off-screen.
- **Two ready Homes:**
  - The Shared House (modern, three floors, ten bedrooms, a new Entrance);
  - The Old Manor (medieval, locked for now).
- **The Home creator:**
  - premise;
  - rooms;
  - bedrooms;
  - cover picture, which can also be one of the room pictures.
- **New game in a Home:**
  - choose the rooms the house has;
  - choose what can be done in each room;
  - add rooms of your own;
  - write your situation, or let your AI write the premise from lorebooks.
- **Floor plan (optional):**
  - one picture per floor, with day, afternoon and night versions;
  - each room drawn as a box: drag to move, drag the corner to resize;
  - floor buttons with the faces of who is on each floor;
  - a red "!" marks rooms not on the plan;
  - without a plan picture, the room pictures are grouped by floor.
- **Time:**
  - the clock moves a few minutes with each message (can be turned off);
  - click the clock to let time pass;
  - sleeping brings you back to where you went to bed.
- **Go out:**
  - write where you are going, pick who comes and a background;
  - the scene out there is free, like a chat;
  - **Back home** asks first.
- **Who lives here:**
  - change bedrooms (two people in one bedroom share it, and the AI knows);
  - someone can move in or move out during the game, with a confirmation.
- **Dress code per room:** an outfit folder from the character's Expressions (swimsuit at the pool, pajamas in
  the bedrooms…). The AI is told what people wear.
- **Housemates:**
  - can go out for a while, or stay in;
  - each can have a job written in your own words, with a shift.
- **House party:** free; everyone leaves their rooms for the common areas.
- **This Home settings:**
  - Bedrooms, Home and Dress code;
  - a new **The house** tab: rooms on or off, what can be done in each, your own rooms, and the floor plan (for
    Homes you made).
- **Difficulty** in a Home changes how fast bonds grow.
- **One long conversation:** moving between rooms or going out no longer hides what was just said from the AI.
  Only sleeping starts a new day.
- **Bathrooms and shower rooms** no longer offer snacks or drinks.
- **Liquid-glass windows:** Let time pass, Go out, Back home, Who lives here and the pickers.

### Porto Alto

- **Pictures:** a background for every place (45) and for six kinds of home:
  - small apartment, apartment, penthouse;
  - small house, house, villa.
- **New places:**
  - **Palm Coast Gym**, apart from the **Stadium**;
  - **Cineplex** in Midtown ("Watch a movie" moved there).
- **The elevator ride** when arriving at an apartment was removed.
- **Map cards:** a place with a long card (many jobs) now shows it beside the pin, so the pin can still be clicked.
- **The HUD on maps:** the clock, the place name and the map tabs never overlap, at any HUD size.

### Life in the world

- **Day and night:**
  - opening hours;
  - prices and the mood of the streets by part of the day;
  - a picture per part of the day for places, homes and maps.
- **Regions:** each has a crowd level, a public mood, tension, weather and prices that drift day by day.
- **People's days:**
  - routines get interrupted (sick at home, errands, working late);
  - places remember regulars;
  - over the weeks, couples get engaged, marry or break up, and people get promoted.
- **Things that come to you:**
  - neighbors knocking;
  - friends asking your advice;
  - news of birthdays, promotions and weddings.
- **City events** (shows, matches, fairs, open mic) and **festivals**, in the calendar.
- **Hobbies** that level up: some jobs ask for a level, and it raises what they pay.
- **Habits that become traits** (Early riser…), and fade when you stop.
- **Other systems** (each can be turned on or off per world in the World creator):
  - reputation per town and secret identities;
  - pets;
  - apprentices;
  - letters from friends in other towns;
  - schools with exams and clubs;
  - factions and guilds;
  - the realm and its gods;
  - trade and smuggling;
  - property, businesses, a garden and loans;
  - taxes;
  - crime and prison;

  edited, and you can add your own instructions.
